# PAS 40 Rudiments: Pedagogical Sequencing, Tempo Benchmarks, and Speed-Building Methods

This document synthesizes how major percussion organizations, graded exam boards, and drum educators sequence the 40 Percussive Arts Society (PAS) International Drum Rudiments, what tempo targets they use, and how drummers build speed cleanly without automated mic/MIDI grading.

---

## 1. Official PAS Classification vs. Pedagogical Sequencing

### 1.1 The Official PAS 40 Taxonomy (Catalog Order)
Adopted in 1984 by a committee chaired by Jay Wanamaker (expanding the original Standard 26 American Drum Rudiments), the PAS 40 International Drum Rudiments are grouped into **four structural families** by stroke type ([PAS](https://www.pas.org/)):

1. **Roll Rudiments (Nos. 1–15)**
   - *Single Stroke Roll Rudiments (1–3):* Single Stroke Roll, Single Stroke Four, Single Stroke Seven
   - *Multiple Bounce Roll Rudiments (4–5):* Multiple Bounce Roll (Buzz Roll), Triple Stroke Roll
   - *Double Stroke Open Roll Rudiments (6–15):* Double Stroke Open Roll, Five, Six, Seven, Nine, Ten, Eleven, Thirteen, Fifteen, and Seventeen Stroke Rolls
2. **Diddle Rudiments (Nos. 16–19):** Single Paradiddle, Double Paradiddle, Triple Paradiddle, Single Paradiddle-diddle
3. **Flam Rudiments (Nos. 20–30):** Flam, Flam Accent, Flam Tap, Flamacue, Flam Paradiddle, Single Flammed Mill, Flam Paradiddle-diddle, Pataflafla, Swiss Army Triplet, Inverted Flam Tap, Flam Drag
4. **Drag Rudiments (Nos. 31–40):** Drag (Ruff), Single Drag Tap, Double Drag Tap, Lesson 25, Single Dragadiddle, Drag Paradiddle #1, Drag Paradiddle #2, Single Ratamacue, Double Ratamacue, Triple Ratamacue

### 1.2 Why Teachers Reject 1–40 Sequential Order
Educators uniformly advise **against** practicing the PAS list straight from #1 to #40, because long/compound rudiments (e.g., 17-Stroke Roll at #15) appear before foundational hand-to-hand patterns (Single Paradiddle at #16, Flam at #20, Drag at #31). Instead, major syllabi use a **spiral/tiered progression** introducing core mechanics from all four families early, then layering derivatives.

#### Vic Firth "40 Essential Rudiments" Learning Sequence (Dr. John Wooton & Mark Wessels)
Vic Firth organizes the 40 rudiments into four progressive tiers ([Vic Firth 40 Essential Rudiments Hub](https://vicfirth.com/blogs/education/40-essential-rudiments-john-wooton)):
- **Tier 1 — Foundation (6 core building blocks):** Single Stroke Roll, Double Stroke Open Roll, Multiple Bounce Roll, Single Paradiddle, Flam, Drag (Ruff).
- **Tier 2 — Expanding the Families:** Short rolls (Five, Seven, Nine Stroke Rolls), Double Paradiddle, Single Paradiddle-diddle, Flam Accent, Flam Tap, Flam Paradiddle, Single Drag Tap, Single Ratamacue.
- **Tier 3 — Intermediate Complexity:** Single Stroke Four & Seven, Triple Stroke Roll, Six/Ten/Eleven/Thirteen/Fifteen/Seventeen Stroke Rolls, Flamacue, Swiss Army Triplet, Pataflafla, Inverted Flam Tap, Single Flammed Mill, Double Drag Tap, Lesson 25, Single Dragadiddle, Double & Triple Ratamacue.
- **Tier 4 — Advanced Mastery & Hybrids:** Triple Paradiddle, Flam Paradiddle-diddle, Flam Drag, Drag Paradiddle #1, Drag Paradiddle #2.

#### Drumeo
Drumeo introduces a **foundational triad** first—Single Stroke Roll (`R L R L`), Double Stroke Roll (`R R L L`), and Single Paradiddle (`R L R R / L R L L`)—before branching into flams, drags, and extended rolls ([Drumeo](https://www.drumeo.com/)).

#### Graded Drum Kit Exam Syllabi (Rockschool, Trinity, LCME)
Contemporary drum kit exam boards introduce rudiments cumulatively across Grades 1–8 and test them both as technical exercises and kit applications:
- **Rockschool (RSL Awards)** ([RSL Drums Syllabus](https://www.rslawards.com/music/graded-music-exams/drums/)):
  - *Grade 1:* Single & double strokes (8ths, 16ths), Single paradiddles (16ths).
  - *Grade 2:* Adds 8th/16th combinations, Flams (quarter notes), 8th-note triplets.
  - *Grade 3:* Adds triplet paradiddles, Flam accents, 5-stroke rolls.
  - *Grade 4:* Adds 16th-note triplets, Double paradiddles, Flam taps, 9-stroke rolls.
  - *Grade 5:* Adds Drags/ruffs, complex flams, 7-stroke rolls.
  - *Grades 6–8:* Adds Ratamacues, combined rolls, and complex tuplets (quintuplets, sextuplets, septuplets, 32nds, nontuplets).
- **Trinity College London** ([Trinity College](https://www.trinitycollege.com/)):
  - *Grade 1:* Single-stroke roll, Double-stroke roll, Single paradiddle.
  - *Grade 3:* Adds Flam, Drag, Four-stroke ruff, Five-stroke roll.
  - *Grade 5:* Adds Seven- & Nine-stroke rolls, Flam tap, Flam accent, Flamacue, Double paradiddle, Paradiddle-diddle, Double drag & stroke, Drag paradiddle, Single/Double/Triple ratamacues.
  - *Grade 8:* Full mastery of all 40 PAS rudiments in stylistic studies.
- **London College of Music (LCME)** ([UWL LCME](https://www.uwl.ac.uk/)):
  - *Grade 1:* Single stroke roll, Double stroke roll, Paradiddle (performed from memory).
  - *Grade 2:* Adds Flams, Drags, Ruffs; scales through Grade 8 with multi-stroke rolls, flam accents, ratamacues, and paradiddle-diddles.

## 2. Published Tempo Benchmarks & Why Flat Ranks Fail

### 2.1 Vic Firth 5-Tier System (Per-Rudiment BPMs)
Vic Firth's "Track Your Progress" system assigns **unique BPM brackets per rudiment** across five ranks: **Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond** ([Vic Firth](https://vicfirth.com/blogs/education/40-essential-rudiments-john-wooton)). Examples reported in Vic Firth progress charts:

| Rudiment | Bronze | Silver | Gold | Platinum | Diamond |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single Stroke Roll** | 44–58 (or 44–56) | 60–72 (58–70) | 74–86 (72–84) | 88–100 (86–98) | 102–114 (100–112+) |
| **Double Stroke Open Roll** | 80–95 | 100–115 | 120–135 | 140–155 | 160–180 |
| **Single Paradiddle** | 44–58 | 60–72 | 74–86 | 88–100 | 102–114 |
| **Single Stroke Four** | 60–84 | 84–108 | 108–132 | 132–156 | 156–180 |
| **Five Stroke Roll** | 60–75 | 80–95 | 100–115 | 120–135 | 140–160 |
| **Flam Accent** | 60–75 | 80–95 | 100–115 | 120–135 | 140–160 |
| **Flam Tap** | 50–65 | 70–85 | 90–105 | 110–125 | 130–150 |
| **Double Ratamacue** | 35–45 | 50–60 | 65–75 | 80–90 | 95–105 |

*Note on subdivision (unverified):* The text summaries of the Vic Firth charts list `m.m.` BPM numbers without explicitly printing the beat-note/subdivision value for each sheet. Based on the 2:1 ratio between Double Stroke Open Roll (Diamond 160–180) and Single Stroke Roll / Single Paradiddle (Diamond 102–114), the Single Stroke Roll and Single Paradiddle charts are almost certainly scored in **32nd notes** (102–114 BPM in 32nds = 204–228 BPM in 16ths), while Double Stroke Open Roll is scored in **16th notes** or half-note pulses; however, the exact notation per chart is **unverified** without viewing the raw PDFs.

### 2.2 Rockschool (RSL) Drum Kit Grade Tempos
Rockschool pairs **moderate BPMs** with **increasingly dense subdivisions** rather than pushing raw BPM linearly ([RSL Syllabus Downloads](https://www.rslawards.com/syllabus-downloads/)):
- **Grade 1:** 70 BPM (8th & 16th notes; metronome optional).
- **Grade 2:** 70–75 BPM (8ths, 16ths, 8th triplets, quarter-note flams).
- **Grade 3:** 75 BPM (8ths, triplets, 16ths, 5-stroke rolls).
- **Grade 4:** 75–80 BPM (metronome **compulsory** from Grade 4+).
- **Grade 5:** 80–90 BPM (drags, complex flams, 5/7/9-stroke roll phrases).
- **Grade 6:** 80 BPM (single/double strokes, paradiddles, ratamacues, rolls).
- **Grade 7:** 65 BPM (single strokes across deep subdivisions), 80 BPM (paradiddles, flams, ratamacues), 85 BPM (7/9-stroke rolls).
- **Grade 8:** 65 BPM (single strokes through quarter, 8th, triplet, 16th, quintuplet, sextuplet, septuplet, 32nd, nontuplet, double quintuplet), 85 BPM (triplets, ratamacues), 90 BPM (paradiddles, combined rolls).

*(Trinity College London does not publish rigid mandatory BPMs per technical exercise, prioritizing evenness and musicality; LCME suggests ~80–100 BPM at Grade 1 and ~92 BPM at Grade 2, scaling upward in higher grades.)*

### 2.3 DCI / Marching Percussion Benchmarks (16th-Note Subdivisions)
Competitive drum corps audition packets evaluate continuous **16th notes** (4 strokes per click) and show clear per-rudiment ceilings:
- **Single Stroke Roll (16ths):** Developing 120–144 BPM | DCI Audition 160–180 BPM | World Class 190–208+ BPM.
- **Double Stroke Roll (16ths):** Developing 132–156 BPM | DCI Audition 172–192 BPM | World Class 200–220+ BPM.
- **Single Paradiddle (16ths):** Developing 104–126 BPM | DCI Audition 144–168 BPM | World Class 172–188+ BPM.

### 2.4 Are Flat Ranks (e.g., Bronze 80 / Silver 120 / Gold 160 / Master 200 BPM) Realistic?
**No.** Flat BPM targets across all 40 rudiments fail for three reasons:
1. **Subdivision ambiguity:** 80 BPM in 32nd notes equals 160 BPM in 16th notes. A rank target is meaningless without specifying the note value (e.g., 16th notes vs. 8th-note triplets).
2. **Biomechanical & stroke-density differences:** A Double Ratamacue contains double-grace-note drags and alternating triplets, capping out around 95–105 BPM even at Vic Firth's Diamond level, whereas an open Double Stroke Roll reaches 160–220 BPM in 16ths. A flat 200 BPM "Master" target is physically unrealistic for dense drag/flam hybrids and trivially slow if played as 8th notes on single strokes.
3. **Control vs. speed:** As Rockschool Grades 7–8 show, playing complex tuplets or clean dynamic accents at 65–90 BPM is a stronger indicator of kit readiness than raw single-stroke velocity.

### 2.5 Proposed Per-Family Tempo Targets for TempoDojo (Home E-Kit Context)
Because TempoDojo targets a **home e-kit drummer** (not a DCI snare audition) and emphasizes groove/feel transfer, targets should be anchored to an explicit **subdivision** (16th notes for duple rudiments, 8th-note triplets or sextuplets for triple rudiments) with four realistic tiers (*Bronze / Silver / Gold / Master*). The table below synthesizes Vic Firth, Rockschool, and DCI benchmarks into kit-practical ranges (exact TempoDojo tier cutoffs are **proposed/synthesized** rather than directly quoted from a single syllabus):

| Rudiment Group | Reference Subdivision | Bronze | Silver | Gold | Master |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single Stroke Roll** | 16th notes | 70 BPM | 100 BPM | 135 BPM | 170+ BPM |
| **Double Stroke Open Roll** | 16th notes | 75 BPM | 110 BPM | 145 BPM | 180+ BPM |
| **Short Rolls (5, 7, 9-Stroke)** | 16th / 32nd roll phrasing | 65 BPM | 90 BPM | 120 BPM | 145+ BPM |
| **Single & Double Paradiddles** | 16th notes (with accents) | 65 BPM | 95 BPM | 125 BPM | 155+ BPM |
| **Paradiddle-diddle / Triple Paradiddle** | 16th / Sextuplet | 60 BPM | 85 BPM | 110 BPM | 135+ BPM |
| **Basic Flams (Flam, Flam Tap)** | 8ths / 16ths | 55 BPM | 80 BPM | 105 BPM | 130+ BPM |
| **Triplet Flams (Flam Accent, Swiss Army)** | 8th-note triplets | 65 BPM | 95 BPM | 125 BPM | 150+ BPM |
| **Complex Flams (Flamacue, Pataflafla, Flam Drag)** | 16ths / Triplets | 55 BPM | 75 BPM | 100 BPM | 125+ BPM |
| **Basic Drags (Drag, Single Drag Tap, Lesson 25)** | 8ths / 16ths | 55 BPM | 75 BPM | 95 BPM | 115+ BPM |
| **Ratamacues & Drag Paradiddles** | Triplets / 16ths | 45 BPM | 65 BPM | 85 BPM | 105+ BPM |

## 3. Accepted Practice Methods for Building Speed Cleanly

### 3.1 Core Speed-Building Protocols
1. **Open-Close-Open (Slow-Fast-Slow):** The official PAS and Drumeo standard—start slow and relaxed, accelerate smoothly to maximum clean speed, then decelerate back to slow control ([PAS](https://www.pas.org/), [Drumeo](https://www.drumeo.com/)).
2. **Step-Tempo / Incremental Overload:** Popularized with George Lawrence Stone's *Stick Control* and Tommy Igoe's *Lifetime Warmup*. Start at a relaxed baseline (e.g., 50–60 BPM) and increase by **2–8 BPM** (Drumeo suggests 5–10 BPM) only after tension-free, clean execution. DCI instructors also use the **"+/- 20 BPM" rule**: to own a tempo, you must play it cleanly 20 BPM faster and 20 BPM slower.
3. **Speed Bursts (Overload Method):** Play 2–4 bars at a comfortable base tempo (70–80 BPM), **burst** to maximum speed for 1–2 beats or 1 full bar, then drop back into the base grid without losing time. Trains fast-twitch mechanics without accumulating lactic acid or tension.
4. **The Pyramid Method:**
   - *Note-Count Pyramid:* Play 1 rep, pause 1 beat; 2 reps, pause 1 beat; up to 8 consecutive reps, then back down to 1.
   - *Tempo Pyramid:* Step up for fixed intervals (e.g., 80 → 90 → 100 → 115 BPM for 15–30 seconds each), then step back down (100 → 90 → 80 BPM). Descending under fatigue forces rebound efficiency rather than muscle force.
5. **Failure-Tempo Diagnostic:** Ascend by 4–5 BPM until technique breaks down (tension, uneven bounces, timing drift), then drop **10–15 BPM below** that failure point for core practice ([Modern Drummer](https://www.moderndrummer.com/)).

### 3.2 The "Clean Rep" Rule & Time at Tempo
- **Streak / Consecutive Clean Reps:** Practice literature commonly prescribes **3, 5, or 10 consecutive clean repetitions** (or measures) before increasing tempo. Any flaw—unintended flamming, uneven stick height, timing drift, or physical tension—resets the streak to zero.
- **Timed Endurance Blocks:** Set a fixed **1- to 2-minute block** at a single BPM (e.g., a 2-minute drill on Double Stroke Rolls or 60-second continuous *Stick Control* loops) to verify consistency and relaxation before bumping +2–4 BPM.

### 3.3 Self-Assessment Without Mic/MIDI Grading
Because TempoDojo relies on self-judgment, the app should guide users through objective self-audit cues:
- **Gap Click / Muted Bars:** Mute the metronome for 1–2 bars out of every 4 bars (directly matching Frozen Ape's *Tempo* Automator). When the click returns, the drummer immediately hears whether they rushed or dragged.
- **Stick Height Discipline:** Enforce distinct height tiers—**1-inch taps** (ghost notes/rolls), **3–6 inch medium strokes**, and **12+ inch accents**. Watching hands in a mirror or phone video exposes weak-hand drop or creeping tap heights.
- **Self-Recording (2-Minute Video/Audio Audit):** Record a 60-second block, step away, and review objectively for symmetry, posture tension, and dynamic evenness.

## Recommendations for TempoDojo

1. **Sequence the 40 rudiments in 4–5 pedagogical tiers, not 1–40:** Group Tier 1 around the Vic Firth / Rockschool core (Single Stroke Roll, Double Stroke Open Roll, Multiple Bounce Roll, Single Paradiddle, Flam, Drag) so beginners build balanced hand mechanics before tackling 17-stroke rolls or hybrid drags.
2. **Always pair BPM targets with an explicit subdivision:** Display both the click BPM and the target note value (e.g., `120 BPM · 16th notes` or `95 BPM · 8th triplets`) in every exercise card to eliminate ambiguity.
3. **Use per-rudiment (or per-family) rank brackets instead of flat BPMs:** Adopt Vic Firth's principle of tailoring Bronze/Silver/Gold/Master BPM ranges to each rudiment's stroke density (e.g., Single Stroke Master at 170+ BPM 16ths vs. Ratamacue Master at 105+ BPM).
4. **Include a "Subdivision Ladder" mode at moderate BPMs:** Inspired by Rockschool Grades 7–8, offer exercises at steady tempos (65–85 BPM) that step through 8ths, triplets, 16ths, and sextuplets—directly fixing weak groove/feel and internal clock stability.
5. **Embed a "Clean Rep" & "2-Minute Endurance" rank-up gate:** Require the user to self-confirm either **5 consecutive clean bars/reps** or a **1–2 minute clean timer block** (zero tension, even stick heights, locked to click) before unlocking the next BPM step (+4–5 BPM) or rank.
6. **Leverage the *Tempo*-style Automator for Speed Bursts and Gap-Click Audits:** Build presets for (a) **Gap-Click Self-Check** (2 bars click / 2 bars muted) as the final rank-up test, and (b) **Tempo Pyramids** (auto-stepping +5 BPM every 30 seconds up and back down).
7. **Add a 3-point self-judgment checklist on the rank-up modal:** Before awarding a rank, prompt the drummer to verify: (1) *Timing* (landed cleanly after muted bars), (2) *Stick Heights* (taps low, accents crisp, L/R balanced), and (3) *Tension* (wrists/fingers relaxed).
8. **Offer a "Kit Transfer" toggle on every rudiment:** Since the target user struggles to transfer pad skills to songs, pair each rudiment level with a brief orchestration cue (e.g., *Paradiddle: RH on hi-hat/ride, LH ghosted on snare*) once Bronze/Silver is cleared.
